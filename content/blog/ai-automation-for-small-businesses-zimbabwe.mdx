Everyone says "AI" right now. Most of it is noise. Here is the simple version.

<Callout title="The short version">

- AI automation means software does a repeat task for you
- Start with one task, not everything
- Fix the process first, then automate it

</Callout>

## What AI automation really is

You do not need a fancy chatbot. It is much simpler than that.

Think of a task your team does every day. Typing invoices. Answering the same questions. Building reports. Software can do that for you.

It works in two parts. First, a trigger, like a new email or a filled form. Second, an AI step, like reading a document or sorting a request. That is what saves hours each week.

<Illustration name="automation" caption="A trigger, an AI step, and it is done" />

## Four tasks to automate first

Pick tasks that happen often, repeat, and eat your team's time. These make the best starters:

1. **Invoices and documents.** AI reads the file and fills in your spreadsheet.
2. **Customer questions.** Prices, stock and opening hours get answered on WhatsApp for you.
3. **Reports.** They build themselves on a schedule.
4. **Reminders and follow-ups.** Appointment and order updates go out without anyone typing.

<Illustration name="chat" caption="Quick answers to the same old questions" />

## How long it takes, and what it costs

One clear task usually takes a few weeks to build. Not months. The price depends on how many tools it must connect to, like WhatsApp, email or your accounting software.

The biggest mistake is trying to do everything at once. Do one task. Get it working. Then add the next.

<Illustration name="clock" caption="Hours won back every week" />

## Three mistakes to avoid

- **Automating a messy process.** It just makes the mess move faster.
- **No human check on money or legal steps.** Always keep one.
- **Picking a tool first.** Pick the task first.

> Fix the process first. Then automate it.
