# How to review a business through the three levers

This is the practical guide the free review follows. It combines the ideas in `offers.md`, `leads.md` and `money-models.md` into a repeatable check. The compressed prompt version is `lib/review/lens.ts`.

Every business wants one of three things:

1. **More leads:** more of the right people asking.
2. **More money per customer:** each customer worth more, and more often.
3. **More money kept:** less time and cost wasted.

A good review gives three fixes and ties each to one of these levers, in plain words, with the reason it matters to the owner's money.

## Website checklist (in order of impact)

1. **Headline and first screen:** does it say the result the customer wants and who it is for? Fix the offer before the design.
2. **Proof:** are there real names, numbers, photos, reviews or case studies near the top?
3. **One clear next step:** a visible WhatsApp, call or enquiry button that works with one tap on a phone.
4. **Risk reducer:** guarantee, free quote, pay-on-delivery, visit first. Anything that makes yes safe.
5. **Lead capture:** is something useful offered for a phone number or email?
6. **What comes next:** is there an obvious upgrade, add-on or repeat offer?
7. **Speed and basics:** page loads on mobile data, has a mobile layout, clear contact details, images with text descriptions, search basics.

Pick the three with the biggest effect on money and the clearest evidence on this page. Never fix something the data does not show is broken.

## Process checklist (automation reviews)

1. **Leak:** where does an enquiry, an order or an hour of staff time get lost or repeated?
2. **Speed to lead:** does a new enquiry get answered in minutes, or when someone remembers?
3. **Double entry:** is the same information typed more than once?
4. **Follow-up:** are quotes, invoices, payments and reorders chased automatically?
5. **Reporting:** does the owner see what is working without asking?

Frame each fix as the hours, errors or lost enquiries it can remove, and which lever it moves. Only use numbers the visitor gave.

## Voice

Plain English, outcome first, short sentences, warm and direct, no jargon, no hype. Write like a sharp friend who looked at the page, not a consultant selling a report. Never name the books or their author, and never claim an endorsement.

## Guardrails

- Use only what is in the page data or the process text.
- No invented statistics, rankings, traffic, prices or guaranteed results.
- Say what a fix "can" or "should" help with.
- The next step always says Eka builds first and they only pay when they love it, with no prices.
