# $100M Money Models: working notes

Our own condensed notes on the ideas in Alex Hormozi's book, written to guide Eka's reviews. They paraphrase the concepts and are not a substitute for reading the book. The compressed version used by the review prompt lives in `lib/review/lens.ts`.

## The core idea

A money model is how a business turns a stranger into a profitable, repeat customer. The business that can afford to spend the most to acquire a customer wins, and the way to afford that is to make each customer worth more in the first weeks, not just eventually.

## Get the money back fast

Aim to recover the cost of acquiring and serving a customer within about 30 days. When the first sale already pays for the marketing, growth is no longer limited by cash. Anything that delays cash (long payment terms, one cheap sale and nothing after) slows the business.

## The four building blocks

1. **Attraction offer:** a low-friction first purchase that brings a new customer in. It may break even or lose a little, as long as the next steps earn it back. Examples: a small paid trial, a discounted starter, a free audit that leads to a paid fix, a bundle.
2. **Upsell:** what the buyer is offered the moment they say yes, or soon after: a bigger version, an add-on, a faster delivery, a done-for-you option.
3. **Downsell:** what to offer someone who says no or "too expensive": a payment plan, a smaller scope, a cheaper version. It rescues sales that would otherwise be lost.
4. **Continuity:** a reason to pay every month: a service plan, subscription, maintenance, a retainer, a membership. This is what makes a customer worth far more over time.

## Ways to raise what each customer is worth

- Offer something after every purchase (the next step is obvious to the customer).
- Bundle and name packages so the choice is simple.
- Anchor with a higher-priced option so the middle one looks reasonable.
- Charge for speed or convenience.
- Reduce churn: make leaving harder and staying valuable (reminders, check-ins, results reports).
- Ask for referrals at the moment the customer is happiest.

## Save money as well as make it

The same lens applies to cost. Look for repeat manual work, double entry, slow replies and mistakes. Each hour or error removed is money kept, and it often pays for itself faster than new marketing.

## What to look for on a small business site or process

- Is there a low-friction first step (free quote, trial, starter package)?
- Is there anything to buy next, or does the relationship end after one sale?
- Is there a recurring offer (care plan, retainer, subscription, repeat order)?
- Does the business make it easy to pay and easy to say yes today?
- In a process: where does time, cash or an enquiry leak away?
