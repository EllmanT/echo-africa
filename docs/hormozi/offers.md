# $100M Offers: working notes

Our own condensed notes on the ideas in Alex Hormozi's book, written to guide Eka's reviews. They paraphrase the concepts and are not a substitute for reading the book. The compressed version used by the review prompt lives in `lib/review/lens.ts`.

## The core idea

People do not buy a service. They buy a better version of their life. An offer is so good that the buyer feels foolish saying no. When the offer is strong, price stops being the main objection, and marketing gets cheaper because more people say yes.

## The Value Equation

Perceived value rises with the first two and falls with the last two:

- **Dream outcome:** how much the buyer wants the result. Name the result, not the activity ("more customers by WhatsApp", not "a website").
- **Perceived likelihood of achievement:** how sure they are it will work for them. Raised by proof, case studies, numbers, guarantees, credentials, specifics.
- **Time delay:** how long until they see a result. Shorter is worth more, including the first small win.
- **Effort and sacrifice:** what it costs them in work, hassle, risk and learning. Less is worth more.

Every review should ask which of the four is weakest on this business's page or process, and fix that one first.

## Pick a market worth serving

A good market has: strong pain, money to spend, is easy to reach, and is growing. Serving everyone means being forgettable. Specific beats generic: a clear "who it is for" line converts better than a broad promise.

## Build the offer

1. List every problem the buyer hits on the way to the dream outcome, including the small annoying ones.
2. Turn each problem into a solution.
3. Decide how it is delivered (done for you, done with you, tools, templates). Done-for-you and fast delivery carry the highest value.
4. Trim what costs a lot and is worth little; stack what costs little and is worth a lot.

## Make it hard to refuse

- **Scarcity and urgency, only when true:** real limits on slots, real deadlines. Fake scarcity destroys trust.
- **Bonuses:** each one removes a specific objection or speeds a result.
- **Guarantees:** reduce the buyer's risk. Unconditional ("if you do not love it, you do not pay") is the strongest. Eka's own "built first, pay only when you love it" is exactly this.
- **Naming:** a clear name that says who it is for and what they get beats a generic service label.

## Pricing

Premium pricing is allowed when the value is clearly higher than the price. Never compete on being cheapest. Show the price against the size of the outcome, not against competitors.

## What to look for on a small business site

- Does the headline state a result a customer wants, or just what the company does?
- Is there proof near the top (numbers, names, photos, reviews)?
- Is it obvious who it is for?
- Is there a risk reducer (guarantee, free trial, free quote, visit first)?
- Is there one clear next step, and does it ask for little effort?
